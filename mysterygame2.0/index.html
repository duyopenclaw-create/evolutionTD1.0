THE AWAKENING — BEHIND THE STORY

CHAPTER 1 — THE VOSS MOTOR INN
🧍
YOU
You wake up with no memory of how you got here. Someone left a pocket watch ticking down. You have one hour to find all the keys and escape the Voss Motor Inn before Cole finds you.
🔪
COLE HARGROVE
The hunter. He moves silently through the 30 rooms, always getting closer. Nobody knows who sent him — or why he's here. He doesn't speak. He doesn't stop. Keep moving.
👴
LORD ALDRIC VOSS (A.V.)
The lord of the estate in 1891. He sealed three servants and a guest in the east wing and covered it up as "structural instability." His confession is hidden somewhere in the house. His initials are monogrammed on everything.
👩
ELEANOR VOSS
Aldric's wife. When she discovered what he'd done, she took all his keys and hid one in each of the 30 rooms — so that any future prisoner could earn their way out. She never left. Her portrait is on the upper landing. She wrote to whoever came next.
🧔
THOMAS
Appears only in a torn photograph with Eleanor in the guest bedroom. "Eleanor & Thomas, before." He is never mentioned by name anywhere else. His fate is unknown.
📖
R. HARWICK
Author of "Secrets of the Voss Estate" (1894). His book is hidden in the library between rotting shelves. He documented the 1891 incident — including what the groundskeeper heard behind the sealed wall.
CHAPTER 2 — THE HACKER'S HOUSE
🧍‍♀️
MARA
Your only ally. She was already inside when you arrived — she doesn't know how she got there either. She's been watched for two years without knowing. She finds a note: MARINA — SLIP 7 — BACKUP. She knows what it means.
💻
THE HACKER
He broadcasts from the roof. Five camera feeds. He knows every room. He's been watching Mara for two years — a photo of her is pinned to the basement wall. He is arrested after you escape. He is never named.
CHAPTER 3 — COUNTY GENERAL HOSPITAL
🧪
DR. VICTOR MOSS
A researcher working on a biological compound he called "Project Anamnesis." He believed it could reverse certain neurological conditions. He was wrong about the dosage. He administered it to his assistant James, then was bitten in return. His field journal is in the Specimen Lab. He never finished it.
🧟
JAMES
Dr. Moss's lab assistant. He volunteered for the first human trial. The journal says he was enthusiastic. He is not enthusiastic any more. He is now somewhere in the lower ward and should not be approached.
👩‍⚕️
NURSE ELLIE T.
She left you the master exit key in the supply closet before she fled. She left a note. She never gave her last name. The supply inventory sheet says she took the south stairs. Nobody knows if she made it.
📋
DR. R. HARWICK
The overnight doctor who filled in your intake form. He left you a note in the patient clipboard explaining the situation. He sealed Ward C himself with duct tape and a trolley. His signature appears on three documents you find in the hospital. He is not found in any of the rooms.
CHAPTER 4 — THE FUTURE HOUSE
🤖
THE MAINTENANCE DRONES
Deployed after the zombie outbreak to clean and sanitise infected zones. Networked to a central hub. They worked perfectly until someone hacked that hub. Now they have new orders. The one in the Arrival Chamber is still charging at 34% when you wake up. The one in the Exit Corridor ran into a wall at full speed and nobody knows why.
💀
THE HACKER (DECEASED)
He compiled everything — the evidence chip, the backup USB, the tablet voice note. He was trying to transmit a kill-switch to shut the drones down. Cole found him first. He left you the codes hidden in each room, and a last message on a cracked tablet on the sofa. He never gave his name. He was never named by anyone else either.
🦾
BIO-UNIT 539
A maintenance unit that was sealed in its shipping container and then wasn't. The container in the Storage Module has been torn open from the inside. The manifest shows it was logged as ACTIVE and last pinged on that floor. It is not in the Storage Module when you arrive. It is somewhere in the house.
CHAPTER 5 — ONE YEAR LATER
👵
MRS KAPOOR
Your downstairs neighbour. She was in her doorway when Cole came looking. She waved you away — go, go. She mouthed: he was tall, dark jacket, very still. He left two minutes ago. She never asked what was happening. At the police station you find Detective Badge 0071: M. Kapoor. She never mentioned it.
📞
THE ANONYMOUS CALLER
Unknown number. Three words in the text message before you even listened to the voicemail: He knows. Run. The voicemail is distorted but familiar — someone connected to the hacker's network who survived. They knew exactly where the evidence chip was and what Cole would do about it. They never called again.
🔪
COLE HARGROVE (ESCAPED)
Convicted. Sentenced. Incarcerated at Blackmore maximum security. Escaped at 11:47pm — killed two guards, left no trail. The police log shows his last confirmed sighting was Grid D7 (east side) — but he was at your building by midnight. Nobody knows how he found you so fast. He doesn't run. He never needed to.
CHAPTER 6 — SPACE ESCAPE
🧍‍♀️
MARA
Five years on. She has a life now — a flat, a job, a therapist she likes. She gets the call at 2am: the alien vessel is broadcasting her name. She doesn't panic. She finds you in the shelter crowd. Her first words: "I know why they're here. We need to go." She boards the rocket with her suit already on. She picked hers up from the trolley first. Her name was on the bag.
💻
THE HACKER (POSTHUMOUS)
He died in hospital three years before the events of Chapter 6. But in his final hours, with access to equipment nobody knew he still had, he cracked alien radio frequencies — signals that had been embedded in background cosmic noise for decades — and transmitted a single name: Mara. He had been obsessed with her since Chapter 2. He never spoke to her. This was his last act. Nobody knows if he knew what he was doing. The server room folder says: TRANSMITTED DATA — ALIEN ORIGIN CONFIRMED.
🛸
THE VESSEL
800 metres across. Triangular. Every light on the hull pulses at a frequency that matches human heartbeats. It arrived on a ballistic trajectory from outside the solar system, decelerating into stable orbit over the Pacific. It has been broadcasting one word since entering the atmosphere. It did not attack. It did not respond to military signals. It waited. When the rocket launched, it watched. Then it left. Nobody knows if it will come back. The military classified everything. The server room folder was scrubbed thirty minutes after you left.
👩‍🚀
THE LAUNCH DIRECTOR
She had both your names on a list before you arrived. When asked about this later, she said: "We had been monitoring the hacker's last broadcast. We traced it. We anticipated." She held two seats for six months. She never told anyone who they were for. She watched the launch through the blast-glass window without blinking. Her last entry in the mission log was two words: They made it.
🔧
THE TECHNICIAN
He was still in the Engineering Bay with his earbuds in when the alien vessel entered atmosphere. He dropped his keycard when he ran. He was later interviewed and said: "I heard something on the speakers — a low hum — and my hands just started shaking. I don't know why. I just ran." He was the last person to leave the facility before the blast doors sealed. He made it.
THE INCIDENT OF 1891
Lord Aldric Voss sealed three servants and one guest inside the east wing of the estate. The official record called it "structural instability." The groundskeeper heard singing from behind the brick wall for three days after the sealing. Aldric wrote his confession and hid it in the room he was most ashamed of — the Secret Room in the attic.
ELEANOR'S GIFT
When Eleanor found out what Aldric had done, she took all his keys and hid one in each of the 30 rooms. Her logic: if any future prisoner opened every door, they had earned the right to leave. The master key she hid last — deep in the ventilation shaft above the attic, only reachable once every other door was open.
THE TALLY WALL
In the Hidden Passage, 29 scratch marks are carved into the wall. Someone was counting. The message on the bathroom mirror — written backwards so it can only be read in a reflection — says: "You are not the first." It was signed by the people of Room 15.
THE VOSS MOTOR INN
The motel sign flickers in the rain: VOSS MOTOR INN — VACANCY. The estate was converted at some point into a motel. The 30 rooms are its 30 chambers. The pocket watch in Room 1 is still ticking. Nobody knows who reset it.
PROJECT ANAMNESIS
Dr. Victor Moss worked for three months on a compound he believed could reverse certain neurological conditions — what he called "persistent memory erasure." His research notes describe successful animal trials. Trial 7 was the first human compound. It was not what he thought it was. His lab was inside County General Hospital, which itself was built on the eastern edge of the old Voss Estate. Some say the ground there has always been strange.
THE CONTAINMENT TIMER
County General's biohazard protocol triggers an automated containment seal — blast doors on all external exits — after a ten-minute warning period. The protocol was designed for chemical spills. It was never designed for what it ended up containing. Once the doors seal, they cannot be opened from the inside or outside without a decontamination team and a 48-hour wait.
THE NETWORK HACK
After the outbreak ended, a global fleet of maintenance drones was deployed to clean and sanitise the affected zones. Every unit ran on a shared network. One central override could control all of them simultaneously. The hacker found the override. He was building a kill-switch when Cole caught him — three minutes before transmission. The drones received one final command instead: Reclassify all biological contacts as threats.
THE FUTURE HOUSE
A prototype residential facility built as a demonstration of automated living. Every room is networked. Every door is code-locked. The evacuation capsule on the roof was a marketing feature — a demonstration of "emergency autonomous egress." Nobody expected it to be the only way out. The hacker hid the door codes throughout the house before he died. Nine rooms. Nine codes. One capsule.
THE EVIDENCE CHIP
The hacker spent three years compiling evidence against Cole Hargrove. Names, dates, locations, financial records, testimony. Everything needed for a permanent conviction. He made two copies: one chip hidden in the office desk drawer, one USB drive wedged into a concrete pillar in an underpass he knew you would have to pass through. He planned for the possibility that he would not survive.
THE GUN ABOLITION ACT
Eight months after the robot incident was resolved, the city council passed the Gun Abolition Act. All privately held firearms were confiscated and destroyed — or were supposed to be. The future-house pistol recovered in the raid was logged as evidence, tagged DO NOT DESTROY, and transferred to Storage Facility Omega for safekeeping. The transfer form was never followed up on. The gun was never destroyed. Nobody filed the paperwork.
COLE'S ESCAPE
Cole Hargrove was held at Blackmore maximum security for eleven months. Two guards died. No CCTV footage showed how he got through the outer perimeter. The investigating officer's report notes one anomaly: every camera in the north corridor experienced a simultaneous 4-second recording gap at 11:43pm. Cole was in his cell at 11:43. He was not at 11:47. The report was never concluded.
THE HACKER'S LAST TRANSMISSION
Three weeks before he died in hospital, the hacker cracked a frequency embedded in cosmic background radiation — a carrier signal that had been repeating since at least 1947. He spent his last functional hours encoding a response. He transmitted a single name. He did not know if anyone was listening. Someone was. The alien vessel changed course seven months later. It had been 2.3 light-years away when it received the signal. It was already coming.
THE ALIEN VESSEL
Military analysis confirmed the vessel decelerated from 0.8% light speed inside the inner solar system — an energy expenditure that should be physically impossible. Every broadcast it transmitted decoded identically: one word, on every frequency humans have ever used. It never responded to outgoing signals. It never threatened. Its hull lights pulsed at 72 beats per minute — the resting human heart rate. When the Falcon Heavy cleared the tower, the vessel adjusted its orbit by exactly 90 degrees and began accelerating outbound. It was gone in four minutes. No one knows where it went. No one knows if it got what it came for. Mara thinks it did.
THE SPACEX EMERGENCY LAUNCH
The Falcon Heavy was already on the pad for a commercial satellite launch when the vessel entered atmosphere. The launch director made a unilateral decision: scrub the payload, load the Dragon capsule, open the manifest to ten civilians. The satellite company sued. The case was dismissed six months later — the judge cited "civilisation-level emergency provisions." The two names at the top of the manifest were classified. The seat reservation dated back six months. Someone knew the vessel was coming long before the public announcement.
THE PRESS BADGE
The journalist who dropped it was accredited for a profile piece on SpaceX's upcoming commercial launch — the satellite one, before the scrub. Her name was not on the emergency manifest. She never got past the first gate. The badge's CLEARANCE LEVEL A was originally intended for press access to the launch pad viewing area. It was never supposed to open a security gate. Nobody updated the access permissions when the emergency was declared. It was an oversight. It saved your life.
THINGS YOU MIGHT NOT HAVE NOTICED
🗝There are 8 regular keys, 2 hammers, 10 treasures, and 1 master key hidden across 30 rooms.
🚪Every single door in the mansion must be opened before the vent yields the master key — Eleanor's rule.
🖼The portrait of Eleanor Voss (Room 17) shows her looking terrified. The note on the back was written in December 1891 — one month after the sealing.
📷The hacker has had a photo of Mara pinned to his basement wall for two years. She never knew.
⏱Cole moves faster as your timer runs low — every 90 seconds with >30 min left, every 60 mid-game, every 30 seconds in the final 10 minutes.
🎵The metronome in the Music Room still ticks when you examine it. The sheet music is "Lament in D Minor" by A. Voss.
🪞The bathroom mirror message is written backwards. It can only be read in a reflection — just like the game tells you.
🏆There are 4 trophy tiers. Platinum requires escaping in under 1 hour — with a 1-hour timer. It's possible.
🛶After escaping chapter 1 you board a boat. The note Mara found — MARINA SLIP 7 — is where it was docked.
👻Eleanor Voss never left the estate. She said so in her letter. The lantern in the Secret Room is still lit.
CHAPTER 3 — THE HOSPITAL
🧪County General Hospital was built on the site of the old Voss Estate east wing. The plaque says "founded 1987." The safe combination is the founding year.
🗝There are 4 keys and 3 treasures across 10 rooms. You have exactly 10 minutes — no extensions.
🧟The thing in the north stairwell camera feed is never named. You see it for exactly one second.
📋Dr. Harwick's intake form admits you were exposed to a biohazard. He signed the form at 03:14am — the same time the containment timer started.
🛡The antidote sample you collect from the security safe is the only vial that existed. It was never administered. Dr. Moss ran out of time.
CHAPTER 4 — THE FUTURE HOUSE
🔢Every code in Chapter 4 comes from something physically in the room — a clock display, a serial number, an emergency sequence, a coordinate prefix. None are random. The hacker chose them deliberately.
🤖BIO-UNIT 539 is never seen directly. Its container is open. The manifest shows it active. It pinged on your floor. Every room you pass through, it has already been.
💎The three Chapter 4 relics — Evidence Chip, Bio-Unit Core, Teleport Crystal — are the hacker's legacy. Each one was placed deliberately. He knew someone would come through after him.
🛸The teleport pad in the Chamber is one-way. The hacker didn't know how to reverse it — only how to use it. The crystal you find in the scorch marks is the only piece of the mechanism that survived intact.
🏠The evacuation capsule's dashboard shows ALL SYSTEMS GO before you've cleared any doors. It was always ready. The biometric lock was the only thing keeping it closed. The emergency override bypasses it entirely — but only if every coded door in the house has been opened first.
CHAPTER 5 — ONE YEAR LATER
🏅Detective Badge 0071 belongs to M. Kapoor. Your neighbour's name is Mrs Kapoor. She was in her doorway watching Cole leave. She never mentioned the badge. She never mentioned anything.
🔌The hacker left a USB drive in the underpass before he died. He knew Cole would track him to the Future House. He hid the backup on a route he calculated you would have to take, one year later. He planned ahead for a future he didn't live to see.
🪖The signal pistol was never meant to fire bullets. It fires a high-intensity flare round — visible at 3km altitude, the operational height of the search helicopter. One shot is enough. You only have one shot.
🚁The search helicopter has been circling for forty minutes before you reach the rooftop. It was dispatched the moment Cole's prison escape was confirmed. Someone expected this would happen. Someone made sure the helicopter was already in the air.
👤If you name yourself Vincent, knocking on locked doors gives a surprise. Vincent is creating a knock knock knock... WAIT WHAT!!!!
🪟The graffiti in the stairwell — KEEP MOVING — is fresh. Mrs Kapoor says she noticed it that evening. Nobody on the floor claims to have painted it. The paint is the same brand as a can found in the alley dumpster behind your building.
CHAPTER 6 — SPACE ESCAPE
🛸The alien vessel was already en route before the hacker sent the transmission. It received the signal and adjusted course — but it had been moving toward this solar system for reasons unknown. The hacker's message just told it where to stop.
🔢The security gate code (839) is visible on a camera feed in the gate room itself. The fuel code (512) is on a laminated card inside a fallen security drone. Neither code is random — they were both in the building before you arrived.
👜Your space suit bag has your name on it in marker. So does Mara's. The launch director had them labelled six months before the vessel arrived. She never told anyone she'd done it.
🪨You can pick up a piece of launch pad gravel on the pad approach. It's the only treasure in Chapter 6 that serves no purpose. Mara judges you for taking it. You take it anyway.
🚀You need three things to launch: the press badge (to get inside), the space suit (to board), and the launch codes (to fire). The badge is found on the floor. The suit has your name on it. The codes were on an unlocked terminal the whole time.
⏱Chapter 6 has the shortest timer of any chapter — 20 minutes for 15 rooms. The fastest recorded completion is under 4 minutes if you know exactly where everything is. The alien vessel is still watching the whole time.
💬In the escape cinematic, Mara says: "He gave them my name to save me. Even dying, he was trying to help." This is the only time anyone in the game says anything kind about the hacker. He is never named. He is never seen. He changed everything.
